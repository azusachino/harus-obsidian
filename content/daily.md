## 📖 Reading Passage

**The Paradox of Modern Innovation**

In our relentless pursuit of technological advancement, we have become enamored with the notion that innovation inevitably begets progress. Yet this presumption warrants scrutiny. Consider the smartphone—a marvel of engineering that has simultaneously liberated and enslaved its users. While it grants unprecedented access to information, it has also fostered an insidious dependency, fragmenting our attention spans and eroding the contemplative space necessary for deep thought.

The philosopher Albert Borgmann articulated this tension through his concept of the "device paradigm." He argued that modern devices promise to deliver commodities—convenience, entertainment, connection—while obscuring the practices and skills they displace. A wood-burning stove, for instance, demands engagement: chopping wood, tending the fire, gathering with family around its warmth. A thermostat, conversely, reduces this rich tapestry of activity to a mere temperature setting, stripping away the focal practices that once anchored domestic life.

This critique extends beyond household appliances to the very architecture of contemporary existence. Algorithms curate our news feeds, effectively constructing echo chambers that reinforce existing biases while masquerading as personalized convenience. We have outsourced not merely physical labor but cognitive effort itself, delegating decisions to systems whose operations remain opaque even to their creators. The ramifications are profound: as we cede agency to machines, we risk atrophying the very faculties—judgment, discernment, wisdom—that distinguish human intelligence.

Yet dismissing all innovation as deleterious would be equally myopic. The challenge lies in cultivating what might be termed "critical adoption"—a discerning engagement with technology that harnesses its benefits while resisting its more pernicious effects. This requires recovering what Borgmann called "focal things and practices": activities that demand our full presence and forge meaningful connections with the world and each other. Whether it be crafting with one's hands, preparing a meal from scratch, or engaging in substantive conversation, these practices serve as bulwarks against the creeping commodification of experience.

## 📝 Grammar Point: Subjunctive Mood in Conditional and Hypothetical Statements

### Explanation

The subjunctive mood expresses wishes, hypotheticals, demands, or suggestions—situations contrary to fact or not yet realized. In modern English, it's most commonly seen in formal contexts and certain fixed expressions. The subjunctive uses the base form of the verb (without conjugation) and "were" instead of "was" for the verb "to be."

**Common uses:**
- After verbs of recommendation, requirement, or suggestion (insist, demand, suggest, require, recommend)
- In "if I were" constructions (contrary-to-fact conditionals)
- In formal "that" clauses expressing necessity or importance
- In certain fixed expressions ("be that as it may," "far be it from me")

### Examples

1. **Recommendation**: The committee recommended that the proposal *be* reviewed by external auditors.
   (Not "is reviewed" or "was reviewed"—subjunctive uses base form)

2. **Contrary-to-fact conditional**: If technology *were* truly liberating, we wouldn't feel so enslaved by our devices.
   (Not "was"—subjunctive uses "were" regardless of person)

3. **Necessity**: It is essential that each citizen *maintain* their capacity for critical thought.
   (Not "maintains"—subjunctive drops the third-person singular "-s")

4. **Suggestion**: I suggest that we *reconsider* our relationship with social media.
   (Not "reconsider" with any auxiliary verb—just the base form)

### Practice

1. Correct if necessary: "The professor insists that every student submits their work on time."

2. Complete the sentence: "If artificial intelligence ____ (be) as intelligent as humans claim, it would understand nuance and context perfectly."

3. Rewrite in formal subjunctive: "The board recommended that the CEO should resign immediately."

## 📝 Grammar Point: Present Tense Regular Verbs (-ar, -er, -ir)

### Explanation

In Spanish, verbs are categorized by their infinitive endings: **-ar**, **-er**, or **-ir**. To conjugate regular verbs in the present tense, remove the infinitive ending and add the appropriate ending for each subject pronoun.

**-AR verbs** (example: hablar - to speak)
- yo hablo (I speak)
- tú hablas (you speak - informal)
- él/ella/usted habla (he/she speaks, you speak - formal)
- nosotros/as hablamos (we speak)
- vosotros/as habláis (you all speak - Spain)
- ellos/ellas/ustedes hablan (they/you all speak)

**-ER verbs** (example: comer - to eat)
- yo como, tú comes, él/ella come, nosotros comemos, vosotros coméis, ellos comen

**-IR verbs** (example: vivir - to live)
- yo vivo, tú vives, él/ella vive, nosotros vivimos, vosotros vivís, ellos viven

### Examples

1. **-AR verb (comprar - to buy)**:
   María compra frutas en el mercado cada sábado.
   *(María buys fruits at the market every Saturday.)*

2. **-ER verb (beber - to drink)**:
   Nosotros bebemos agua fresca todos los días.
   *(We drink fresh water every day.)*

3. **-IR verb (escribir - to write)**:
   Yo escribo correos electrónicos en español.
   *(I write emails in Spanish.)*

4. **Multiple verbs**:
   Tú hablas español, comes comida mexicana y vives en Madrid.
   *(You speak Spanish, eat Mexican food, and live in Madrid.)*

## 📝 Grammar Point: 敬語表現「〜でございます」と「〜(さ)せていただく」の使い分け

### Explanation

丁寧語の「〜でございます」と謙譲語の「〜(さ)せていただく」は、ビジネスや正式な場面で頻繁に使われる敬語表現ですが、適切な使い分けが重要です。

**〜でございます**
- 「です」の最も丁寧な形
- 丁寧語として、話し手が聞き手に対して敬意を示す
- 名詞や形容動詞に接続
- 使用例: 「こちらが会場でございます」「誠に申し訳ございません」

**〜(さ)せていただく**
- 謙譲語として、自分の動作をへりくだって表現
- 相手の許可や恩恵を受けて行う行為に使用
- 動詞の未然形に「せていただく」を付ける（五段動詞）、または「させていただく」（上一段・下一段動詞）
- **注意**: 過剰使用は不自然。相手の許可や恩恵が関係ない場合は「いたします」を使う
- 適切な例: 「本日は休ませていただきます」（許可が必要）
- 不適切な例: 「説明させていただきます」→「説明いたします」の方が自然

### Examples

1. **でございます（丁寧語）**:
   こちらが新商品のカタログでございます。どうぞご覧くださいませ。
   *This is the catalog for our new products. Please take a look.*

2. **させていただく（謙譲語・適切な使用）**:
   お客様のご要望により、配送日を変更させていただきました。
   *At the customer's request, we changed the delivery date.*
   （相手の要望という恩恵を受けている）

3. **いたします（過剰な「させていただく」を避ける）**:
   ×: それでは、会議を開始させていただきます。
   ○: それでは、会議を開始いたします。
   *Now, we will begin the meeting.*
   （特に許可や恩恵がないため、「いたします」が適切）

4. **複合的な使用**:
   誠に恐れ入りますが、本日は休業日でございます。明日、改めてご連絡させていただきます。
   *We apologize, but today is a holiday. We will contact you again tomorrow.*

### Practice

1. 次の文を適切な敬語表現に直しなさい：
   「これは私の意見です。明日、また来ます。」（顧客への説明として）

2. 次の文の誤りを指摘し、修正しなさい：
   「それでは、プレゼンテーションを始めさせていただきます。」

3. 「でございます」を使って、自社の新製品を紹介する文を作りなさい。

2. 山田氏の哲学は、一つの菓子に季節の移ろいを封じ込めることを使命とし、「急がば回れ」の精神で、時間をかけて丁寧に作品を仕上げることである。そこには謙虚さと職人としての誇りが共存している。

3. 「一期一会」の精神は、同じ材料・手順でも、その時々の条件や職人の心持ちによって仕上がりが異なるという考え方と結びついている。そのため、一つ一つの作品が唯一無二の存在となり、その瞬間を大切にする姿勢につながる。

4. 「暗黙知」とは、言葉やマニュアルで説明できない、体験を通じて身につける知識のこと。職人技は何十年もかけて体得するもので、弟子は師匠の所作を観察し、失敗を重ねながら徐々に習得する。この過程が人格形成にも関わるため、テクノロジーでは代替できない重要な要素である。

5. 筆者は、効率性を追求するあまり、人間にとって本質的なもの（時間をかけた丁寧な仕事、人格形成のプロセス、唯一無二の価値など）を失ってしまうのではないかという懸念を示している。
